# Growing Roses: A Beginner's Guide

## Introduction

Roses have a reputation for being difficult, but with the right variety and basic care, anyone can grow beautiful roses. Modern disease-resistant cultivars have made rose gardening more accessible than ever.

## Choosing the Right Roses

- **Floribunda:** Clusters of blooms, excellent for borders. Very forgiving for beginners.
- **Hybrid Tea:** Classic long-stemmed roses. Require a bit more care.
- **Shrub Roses:** Hardy, disease-resistant, and low-maintenance. Great first choice.
- **Climbing Roses:** Perfect for trellises, fences, and arbors.
- **Knock Out® Roses:** Specifically bred for disease resistance and continuous blooming.

## Planting

- **When:** Early spring (after last frost) or fall (6 weeks before first frost).
- **Location:** Full sun — at least 6 hours of direct sunlight daily.
- **Spacing:** 2–3 feet apart for most varieties to ensure good air circulation.
- **Soil:** Rich, well-drained, slightly acidic (pH 6.0–6.5).

### Planting Steps

1. Dig a hole twice as wide and just as deep as the root ball.
2. Mix compost into the excavated soil.
3. Place the rose so the graft union (the bulge near the base) sits at or just above soil level.
4. Backfill, tamp gently, and water deeply.
5. Apply 2–3 inches of mulch, keeping it away from the stem.

## Ongoing Care

- **Water:** 1–2 inches per week. Water at the base to keep foliage dry and prevent fungal disease.
- **Fertilize:** Start in spring when new growth appears. Use a balanced rose fertilizer every 4–6 weeks.
- **Prune:** In late winter or early spring, remove dead or crossing branches. Cut at a 45° angle above an outward-facing bud.
- **Deadhead:** Remove spent blooms to encourage reblooming throughout the season.

> **Warning:** Watch for black spot and powdery mildew. Ensure good air circulation and avoid overhead watering.
