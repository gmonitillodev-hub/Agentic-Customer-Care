# Growing Strawberries in Pots and Containers

## Introduction

You don't need a large garden to enjoy fresh, homegrown strawberries. Container growing is perfect for patios, balconies, and small spaces — and it keeps fruit off the ground, reducing pest and disease problems.

## Choosing Varieties

- **June-bearing:** One large harvest in early summer. Best for preserves and freezing.
- **Everbearing:** Two to three harvests per season (spring, summer, fall).
- **Day-neutral:** Produce fruit continuously throughout the growing season. Ideal for containers.

**Recommended container varieties:** 'Albion,' 'Seascape,' 'Tristar,' 'Mara des Bois.'

## Container Selection

- Use pots at least **10–12 inches wide** and **8 inches deep**.
- Strawberry towers, hanging baskets, and tiered planters maximize space.
- **Drainage holes are essential** — strawberries are prone to root rot in waterlogged soil.
- One plant per 10-inch pot, or 3–4 plants in a large 18-inch container.

## Soil & Planting

- Use a high-quality **potting mix** with added perlite for drainage.
- Slightly acidic soil (pH 5.5–6.8) is ideal.
- Plant with the **crown at soil level** — burying it causes rot; planting too high exposes roots.

## Care & Maintenance

- **Sunlight:** 6–8 hours of full sun daily.
- **Watering:** Keep soil consistently moist but never soggy. Containers dry out faster than garden beds — check daily in hot weather.
- **Fertilizing:** Feed every 2–3 weeks with a balanced liquid fertilizer during the growing season. Reduce in winter.
- **Runners:** Pinch off runners to direct energy into fruit production, unless you want new plants.

## Winter Care

- In cold climates, move containers to a sheltered location (unheated garage or against a south-facing wall).
- Insulate pots with burlap or bubble wrap to prevent root freeze.
- Reduce watering but don't let soil dry out completely.

> **Tip:** Replace container strawberry plants every 2–3 years, as productivity declines with age.
