# Beginner's Guide to Composting at Home

## Introduction

Composting is nature's recycling system — transforming kitchen scraps and yard waste into nutrient-rich humus that your garden will love. It reduces landfill waste, saves money on soil amendments, and improves your garden soil's structure, fertility, and water-holding capacity.

## What You Need

- A **compost bin, tumbler, or designated pile area** (even a corner of the yard works).
- **Brown materials** (carbon-rich): Dried leaves, straw, cardboard, newspaper, wood chips, dryer lint.
- **Green materials** (nitrogen-rich): Fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, tea bags, fresh grass clippings, plant trimmings.
- **Water** and **air** to keep the process moving.

## The Golden Ratio

Aim for approximately **3 parts browns to 1 part greens** by volume. This carbon-to-nitrogen balance is what fuels efficient decomposition.

- Too many greens → Slimy, smelly pile (anaerobic)
- Too many browns → Very slow decomposition

## How to Build Your Pile

1. **Start with a layer of coarse browns** (twigs, straw) at the bottom for airflow.
2. **Alternate layers** of greens and browns, each 2–4 inches thick.
3. **Moisten each layer** — the pile should be as damp as a wrung-out sponge.
4. **Turn the pile** every 1–2 weeks to introduce oxygen and speed decomposition.
5. **Finished compost** is dark, crumbly, and earthy-smelling — typically ready in 2–6 months.

## What to Compost

- ✅ Fruit and vegetable scraps
- ✅ Coffee grounds and paper filters
- ✅ Eggshells (crushed)
- ✅ Leaves, grass clippings, and garden waste
- ✅ Shredded newspaper and cardboard

## What NOT to Compost

- ❌ Meat, fish, and bones
- ❌ Dairy products
- ❌ Cooking oils and grease
- ❌ Pet waste
- ❌ Diseased plants or weeds with seeds
- ❌ Chemically treated wood or plants

## Troubleshooting

| Problem              | Cause                    | Solution                          |
|----------------------|--------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Bad smell            | Too wet / too many greens| Add browns, turn pile             |
| Not decomposing      | Too dry / too many browns| Add water and greens, turn pile   |
| Attracting pests     | Exposed food scraps      | Bury greens in center of pile     |
| Pile is cold         | Too small or needs turning| Build pile to 3×3×3 ft minimum, turn regularly |

> **Tip:** Keep a small countertop compost pail in your kitchen to collect daily scraps conveniently. Empty it into your outdoor bin every few days.
