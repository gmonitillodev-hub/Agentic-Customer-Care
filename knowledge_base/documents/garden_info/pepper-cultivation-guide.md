# Growing Hot and Sweet Peppers

## Introduction

Peppers are warm-season crops that thrive in heat and sunshine. From sweet bell peppers to fiery habaneros, they're rewarding to grow and incredibly versatile in the kitchen. With the right conditions, a single plant can produce dozens of peppers throughout the season.

## Popular Varieties

### Sweet Peppers
- **Bell peppers:** Classic choice — green, red, yellow, or orange.
- **Sweet banana peppers:** Mild, great for frying and pickling.
- **Shishito:** Mild Japanese variety, perfect for blistering in a hot pan.

### Hot Peppers
- **Jalapeño:** Medium heat (2,500–8,000 SHU). Very productive.
- **Cayenne:** Hot and thin-walled, excellent for drying.
- **Habanero:** Very hot (100,000–350,000 SHU). Fruity flavor.
- **Ghost pepper (Bhut Jolokia):** Extremely hot. For experienced growers and adventurous eaters.

## Starting Seeds

- Start indoors **8–10 weeks before last frost** — peppers need a long growing season.
- Germination temperature: **80–85°F (27–29°C).** Use a heat mat for best results.
- Germination time: 7–21 days (hot peppers are often slower than sweet varieties).

## Transplanting

- Harden off seedlings for 7–10 days before moving outdoors.
- Transplant when nighttime temperatures consistently stay above **55°F (13°C)**.
- Space plants **18–24 inches apart** in full sun.
- Peppers love warm soil — black plastic mulch can help raise soil temperature.

## Care

- **Water:** 1–2 inches per week. Consistent moisture prevents blossom end rot.
- **Fertilize:** Use a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer once flowering begins. Too much nitrogen produces leaves at the expense of fruit.
- **Support:** Stake or cage heavy-producing plants to prevent branches from breaking.

## Harvesting

- Sweet peppers: Harvest at any color stage, but flavor and nutrition improve as they ripen.
- Hot peppers: Heat level increases as peppers mature and change color.
- Use pruning shears — pulling can damage the plant.

> **Warning:** Always wear gloves when handling hot peppers, and avoid touching your eyes. Capsaicin oils can cause severe irritation.
