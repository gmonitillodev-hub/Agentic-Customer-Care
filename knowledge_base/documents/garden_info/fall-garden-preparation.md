# Preparing Your Garden for Autumn and Winter

## Introduction

Fall is not the time to abandon your garden — it's the time to set the stage for a thriving spring. A few hours of autumn preparation can save you weeks of work when the growing season returns.

## Clean Up

- **Remove spent annuals** and diseased plant material. Do not compost diseased plants — dispose of them to prevent pathogen spread.
- **Cut back perennials** that have finished blooming, but leave ornamental grasses and plants with seed heads for winter interest and wildlife habitat.
- **Rake fallen leaves** from lawns (excess leaves smother grass), but use them as free mulch for garden beds.

## Soil Care

- **Test your soil** in fall — it gives you time to amend before spring planting.
- **Add compost** or aged manure to beds. Winter weather will help incorporate it naturally.
- **Plant a cover crop** (winter rye, crimson clover, or field peas) in empty vegetable beds to prevent erosion and add nutrients.

## Planting

- **Plant spring-blooming bulbs:** Tulips, daffodils, crocus, and hyacinths (see our Spring Bulb Planting Guide).
- **Plant garlic:** Fall is the ideal time for garlic planting.
- **Transplant trees and shrubs:** Fall planting allows root establishment before winter dormancy.

## Lawn Care

- **Aerate compacted lawns** to improve water and nutrient penetration.
- **Overseed bare patches** with cool-season grass seed.
- **Apply fall fertilizer** to strengthen root systems before winter.

## Protect Tender Plants

- **Mulch heavily** around the base of tender perennials and newly planted trees.
- **Wrap young trees** with tree guards to prevent frost cracking and rodent damage.
- **Bring tropical plants indoors** before nighttime temperatures dip below 50°F (10°C).

## Tool Maintenance

- Clean, sharpen, and oil garden tools before storing for winter.
- Drain hoses and shut off outdoor water sources to prevent freezing.

> **Tip:** Fall leaves are gardening gold. Shred them with a mower and use as mulch or add to your compost bin as a carbon-rich "brown" layer.
