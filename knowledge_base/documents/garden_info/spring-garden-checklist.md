# Essential Tasks for Spring Garden Startup

## Introduction

Spring is the most exciting — and busiest — time in the garden. This checklist will help you prioritize tasks and ensure you don't miss critical timing windows for planting, pruning, and preparation.

## Early Spring (As Snow Melts)

- [ ] **Clean up winter debris:** Remove dead leaves, broken branches, and old mulch from beds.
- [ ] **Inspect structures:** Check fences, trellises, raised beds, and cold frames for winter damage.
- [ ] **Service tools:** Sharpen pruners, hoes, and mower blades. Replace worn handles.
- [ ] **Order seeds and plants:** Don't wait — popular varieties sell out fast.
- [ ] **Start seeds indoors:** Tomatoes, peppers, and eggplant 6–8 weeks before last frost.

## Mid-Spring (Soil is Workable)

- [ ] **Test soil:** Check pH and nutrient levels. Amend as needed with lime, sulfur, or compost.
- [ ] **Prepare beds:** Turn soil lightly, incorporate compost, and rake smooth.
- [ ] **Direct sow cool-season crops:** Peas, spinach, lettuce, radishes, and carrots can handle light frost.
- [ ] **Plant bare-root trees, shrubs, and roses** while still dormant.
- [ ] **Divide perennials:** Split overcrowded hostas, daylilies, ornamental grasses, and other perennials.
- [ ] **Prune summer-blooming shrubs:** Butterfly bush, hydrangea (paniculata types), and roses.

## Late Spring (After Last Frost)

- [ ] **Transplant warm-season seedlings:** Tomatoes, peppers, squash, cucumbers.
- [ ] **Direct sow warm-season seeds:** Beans, corn, sunflowers, zinnias.
- [ ] **Install irrigation systems:** Drip lines and soaker hoses before plants grow large.
- [ ] **Apply fresh mulch:** 2–3 inches to suppress weeds and retain moisture.
- [ ] **Set up supports:** Install tomato cages, bean poles, and trellises at planting time.

## Ongoing Spring Tasks

- [ ] **Monitor for pests:** Aphids, slugs, and cutworms are active in spring.
- [ ] **Weed regularly:** Young weeds are easiest to pull. Stay on top of them weekly.
- [ ] **Harden off indoor seedlings** gradually before transplanting outdoors.

> **Tip:** Keep a garden journal. Note planting dates, varieties, weather conditions, and what works — it's your most valuable tool year after year.
