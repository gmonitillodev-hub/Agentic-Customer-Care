# Starting a Vegetable Garden in Raised Beds

## Introduction

Raised beds are one of the best ways to start a productive vegetable garden. They offer better drainage, warmer soil, fewer weeds, and complete control over your soil quality — even if your native soil is poor.

## Benefits of Raised Beds

- **Improved drainage** prevents waterlogged roots
- **Soil warms faster** in spring, extending your growing season
- **Reduced weed pressure** compared to in-ground gardens
- **Easier on your back and knees** — less bending required
- **Better pest control** — easier to add barriers and covers

## Choosing Materials

- **Cedar or redwood:** Naturally rot-resistant, lasts 10–15 years.
- **Galvanized steel:** Durable, modern look, excellent longevity.
- **Composite lumber:** Recycled material, no chemicals, long-lasting.

> **Warning:** Avoid pressure-treated lumber, railroad ties, or painted wood — chemicals can leach into soil and contaminate edibles.

## Ideal Dimensions

- **Width:** 3–4 feet (allows you to reach the center from either side)
- **Length:** 6–8 feet is most common, but any length works
- **Depth:** Minimum 10–12 inches; 18–24 inches for deep-rooted crops like carrots and tomatoes

## Filling Your Raised Beds

Use the following layered approach for best results:

1. **Bottom layer (if bed is 18+ inches):** Logs, branches, or straw for bulk and drainage
2. **Middle layer:** Aged compost and garden soil mixed 50/50
3. **Top layer:** Premium garden soil blended with compost and worm castings

## Best Vegetables for Raised Beds

- Tomatoes, peppers, and eggplant
- Lettuce, spinach, and kale
- Carrots, radishes, and beets
- Beans, peas, and cucumbers
- Herbs like basil, cilantro, and dill

## Key Tips

- **Rotate crops** annually to prevent nutrient depletion and disease.
- **Amend soil** each season by top-dressing with 1–2 inches of compost.
- **Install drip irrigation** for efficient, consistent watering.
- **Use trellises** for climbing vegetables to maximize space.
