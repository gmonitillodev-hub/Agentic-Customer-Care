# Growing Tomatoes from Seed: A Complete Guide

## Introduction

Growing tomatoes from seed gives you access to hundreds of varieties beyond what's available as transplants at your local nursery. With a little patience and the right technique, you can enjoy a bountiful harvest of homegrown tomatoes.

## When to Start

- **Indoors:** Start seeds 6–8 weeks before your last expected frost date.
- **Soil temperature for transplanting:** At least 60°F (15°C).

## Materials Needed

- Tomato seeds (choose determinate for containers, indeterminate for larger gardens)
- Seed-starting mix (sterile and lightweight)
- Seed trays or small pots with drainage holes
- Grow lights or a bright south-facing window
- Spray bottle for misting

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Fill trays** with moistened seed-starting mix to about ¾ full.
2. **Sow seeds** ¼ inch deep, spacing them 1 inch apart.
3. **Cover lightly** with mix and mist gently.
4. **Cover trays** with a humidity dome or plastic wrap to retain moisture.
5. **Maintain temperature** between 70–80°F (21–27°C) for optimal germination.
6. **Remove covers** once seedlings emerge (typically 5–10 days).
7. **Provide light:** 14–16 hours per day under grow lights, keeping lights 2–3 inches above seedlings.

## Transplanting Outdoors

- **Harden off** seedlings over 7–10 days by gradually increasing outdoor exposure.
- **Plant deeply** — bury up to two-thirds of the stem to encourage strong root development.
- **Space plants** 24–36 inches apart depending on variety.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Overwatering:** Soggy soil causes damping-off disease. Keep soil moist but not waterlogged.
- **Insufficient light:** Leggy seedlings result from too little light.
- **Skipping hardening off:** Transplant shock can set plants back weeks.

> **Tip:** Rotate your tomato planting location each year to reduce the risk of soil-borne diseases.
