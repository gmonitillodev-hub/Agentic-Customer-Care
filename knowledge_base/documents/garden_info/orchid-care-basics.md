# Basic Orchid Care and Maintenance

## Introduction

Orchids are often perceived as difficult, but the most common variety — the Phalaenopsis (moth orchid) — is actually one of the easiest houseplants to maintain. With proper light, watering, and a little patience, orchids will reward you with stunning blooms that last for months.

## Light

- **Phalaenopsis:** Bright, indirect light. An east-facing window is perfect.
- **Cattleya and Dendrobium:** Need more light — south or west-facing windows with filtered light.
- **Signs of too much light:** Yellowed or sunburned leaves.
- **Signs of too little light:** Dark green leaves (healthy orchid leaves are medium olive green) and failure to bloom.

## Watering

Overwatering is the most common cause of orchid death.

- **Frequency:** Once every 7–10 days. Water more often in summer, less in winter.
- **Method:** Place the pot under lukewarm running water for 15–30 seconds, allowing it to drain completely. Never let the pot sit in standing water.
- **Ice cube method:** While popular, it's not ideal. Room-temperature water is better for tropical plants.
- **Check roots:** Healthy roots are firm and green (when wet) or silvery-white (when dry). Brown, mushy roots indicate rot.

## Humidity & Temperature

- Orchids prefer **50–70% humidity.** Use a humidity tray or group plants together.
- Ideal temperature: **65–80°F (18–27°C)** during the day, with a slight nighttime drop.
- A 10–15°F temperature drop at night can actually trigger blooming in reluctant orchids.

## Potting & Repotting

- Use **orchid bark mix** — never regular potting soil, which suffocates roots.
- Repot every **1–2 years** or when the bark has decomposed and no longer drains well.
- Choose a pot with ample drainage. Clear pots let you monitor root health.

## Fertilizing

- Feed with a balanced orchid fertilizer (20-20-20) diluted to half strength.
- **"Weekly, weakly"** is the mantra — fertilize lightly with each watering during the growing season.
- Skip fertilizer during dormant periods and when in full bloom.

## Reblooming

After flowers drop, cut the spike above the second or third node from the base. Place in bright indirect light with a nighttime temperature drop, and new blooms may appear in 2–3 months.

> **Tip:** Aerial roots growing outside the pot are completely normal and healthy — don't trim them.
