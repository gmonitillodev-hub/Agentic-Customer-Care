# Setting Up an Indoor Herb Garden

## Introduction

Fresh herbs at your fingertips — no garden required. An indoor herb garden is perfect for apartment dwellers, winter growers, or anyone who loves cooking with the freshest ingredients possible.

## Best Herbs for Indoor Growing

- **Basil:** Loves warmth and bright light. Pinch flowers to encourage bushy growth.
- **Mint:** Extremely vigorous — always grow in its own container to prevent it from taking over.
- **Rosemary:** Prefers bright light and well-drained soil. Avoid overwatering.
- **Chives:** Low-maintenance and tolerant of lower light conditions.
- **Parsley:** Slow to germinate but reliable once established.
- **Thyme:** Compact growth habit, ideal for sunny windowsills.

## Essential Requirements

### Light
- Most herbs need **6–8 hours of direct sunlight** per day.
- A south-facing window is ideal. Supplement with **full-spectrum grow lights** if natural light is insufficient.
- Keep grow lights on for 12–16 hours daily, positioned 6–12 inches above plants.

### Containers
- Use pots with **drainage holes** — herbs despise sitting in water.
- Terracotta pots are excellent because they wick away excess moisture.
- Minimum pot size: 6 inches in diameter per herb.

### Soil
- Use a **well-draining potting mix** — never garden soil, which compacts in containers.
- Adding perlite (20–30%) improves drainage significantly.

## Watering Tips

- Water when the top inch of soil feels dry.
- Herbs like rosemary and thyme prefer to dry out slightly between waterings.
- Basil and mint prefer consistently moist (but not soggy) soil.

## Harvesting

- Begin harvesting once plants have at least **6–8 sets of leaves**.
- Never remove more than **one-third** of the plant at a time.
- Regular harvesting encourages bushier, more productive growth.

> **Tip:** Group herbs by water needs — place mint and basil together, rosemary and thyme together.
