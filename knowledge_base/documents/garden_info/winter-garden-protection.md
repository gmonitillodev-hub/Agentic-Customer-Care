# Protecting Plants from Frost and Cold

## Introduction

Winter doesn't have to mean garden devastation. With the right protective strategies, you can help perennials survive harsh cold, extend the season for cool-weather crops, and ensure your landscape emerges healthy in spring.

## Understanding Frost

- **Light frost (29–32°F / -2 to 0°C):** Damages tender annuals and tropical plants.
- **Hard frost (below 28°F / -2°C):** Can damage or kill many perennials and semi-hardy plants.
- **Frost pockets:** Low-lying areas where cold air settles. Avoid planting tender species here.

## Mulching for Insulation

- Apply **4–6 inches of mulch** around the base of perennials, shrubs, and trees after the ground freezes.
- Use straw, shredded leaves, pine needles, or wood chips.
- Mulch insulates roots against temperature fluctuations — the goal is to keep the ground consistently frozen, not warm.
- Pull mulch back gradually in spring as temperatures rise.

## Covering Plants

- **Frost blankets / row covers:** Lightweight fabric that traps heat radiating from the soil. Can provide 4–8°F of protection.
- **Burlap screens:** Excellent for protecting evergreens from desiccating winter winds.
- **Cloches and cold frames:** Extend the growing season for cold-hardy vegetables.
- **Never use plastic directly on plants** — it conducts cold and causes freeze damage where it touches foliage.

## Wrapping and Shielding

- **Wrap young tree trunks** with tree wrap to prevent sunscald (freeze-thaw cracking on south-facing bark).
- **Protect grafted roses** by mounding 10–12 inches of soil or mulch around the graft union.
- **Shield broadleaf evergreens** (rhododendrons, boxwood) from wind with burlap windbreaks.

## Watering Before Frost

- **Water the ground thoroughly** before a hard freeze — moist soil holds more heat than dry soil and protects roots.
- Hydrated plants are more resistant to frost damage than drought-stressed ones.

## Container Plants

- Move containers to sheltered spots (against south-facing walls, under eaves).
- Group pots together for mutual insulation.
- Wrap pots in bubble wrap or burlap to protect roots.

> **Warning:** Don't prune frost-damaged plants immediately. Wait until spring growth begins to assess actual damage — many plants recover from branches that appear dead.
