# How to Care for Succulents and Cacti

## Introduction

Succulents and cacti are among the most popular houseplants, prized for their striking forms, low water needs, and forgiving nature. However, "low maintenance" doesn't mean "no maintenance." Understanding their basic needs is key to keeping them healthy and thriving.

## Light Requirements

- **Most succulents:** Need at least **6 hours of bright, indirect light** daily.
- **Cacti:** Prefer **direct sunlight** — a south or west-facing window is ideal.
- **Signs of too little light:** Stretching (etiolation), pale color, leaning toward the light source.
- **Signs of too much light:** Brown or white sunburn patches on leaves.
- Rotate pots weekly for even growth.

## Watering

This is where most people go wrong. The golden rule: **soak and dry.**

1. Water thoroughly until water flows from the drainage holes.
2. Allow the soil to **dry completely** before watering again.
3. **Frequency:** Every 7–14 days in summer, every 3–4 weeks in winter.
4. Never let succulents sit in standing water.

> **Warning:** More succulents die from overwatering than any other cause. When in doubt, wait a few more days.

## Soil

- Use a **fast-draining succulent or cactus mix**.
- Improve drainage further by mixing in perlite or coarse sand (50/50 ratio).
- Never use regular potting soil alone — it retains too much moisture.

## Containers

- **Always use pots with drainage holes.** Decorative pots without holes are risky.
- Terracotta is ideal — porous material wicks away excess moisture.
- Choose a pot only slightly larger than the plant to prevent excess soil moisture.

## Common Issues

- **Mushy, translucent leaves:** Overwatering or root rot.
- **Wrinkled, shriveled leaves:** Underwatering — give a good soak.
- **White cottony spots:** Mealybugs — treat with rubbing alcohol on a cotton swab.
- **Black stem base:** Root rot — remove affected parts and repot in dry soil.

## Propagation

Many succulents are easy to propagate from leaf cuttings or offsets. Simply let a healthy leaf dry for 2–3 days, then place on moist soil. Roots will appear in 2–4 weeks.
