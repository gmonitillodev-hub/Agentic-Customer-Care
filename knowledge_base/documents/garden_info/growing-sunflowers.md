# Guide to Growing Sunflowers from Seed

## Introduction

Sunflowers are the quintessential symbol of summer — bold, cheerful, and surprisingly easy to grow. Whether you want towering giants for the back of a border or compact varieties for containers, sunflowers deliver dramatic results with minimal effort.

## Choosing Varieties

- **Giant varieties:** 'Mammoth Russian,' 'American Giant' — grow 8–12 feet tall with large seed heads.
- **Branching varieties:** 'Autumn Beauty,' 'Velvet Queen' — multiple blooms per plant in warm autumn colors.
- **Dwarf/container varieties:** 'Teddy Bear,' 'Sunspot,' 'Little Becka' — 1–3 feet tall, perfect for pots.
- **Pollenless varieties:** 'ProCut' series — ideal for cut flowers, no pollen mess on furniture.

## When and Where to Plant

- **When:** Direct sow after all danger of frost has passed and soil temperature reaches 50°F (10°C).
- **Where:** Full sun — they need a minimum of 6–8 hours of direct sunlight. Choose a sheltered spot for tall varieties to protect from wind.

## Planting Instructions

1. **Sow seeds** directly in the ground, 1 inch deep and 6 inches apart.
2. **Thin seedlings** to 12–24 inches apart once they have two sets of true leaves (spacing depends on variety).
3. **For continuous blooms,** sow a new batch every 2 weeks through early summer.

## Care

- **Water:** Deeply but infrequently — about 1 inch per week. Water at the base of the plant.
- **Fertilize:** Sunflowers aren't heavy feeders. Over-fertilizing causes weak stems. A light application of balanced fertilizer at planting is sufficient.
- **Staking:** Tall varieties may need support. Install stakes early to avoid damaging roots later.

## Harvesting Seeds

- Seeds are ready when the back of the flower head turns brown and the seeds look plump.
- Cut the head with 12 inches of stem, hang upside down in a dry area, and rub seeds free once fully dry.

> **Tip:** Sunflowers are allelopathic — they release substances that can inhibit nearby plant growth. Give them their own space or plant along borders.
