# How to Clean, Sharpen, and Store Garden Tools

## Introduction

Well-maintained tools make gardening easier, more efficient, and more enjoyable. A sharp, clean tool cuts cleaner (reducing plant damage and disease spread), requires less effort, and lasts for decades. A few minutes of care after each use pays off enormously.

## Cleaning

### After Every Use
1. **Knock off loose soil** by tapping tools against a hard surface.
2. **Wipe blades and metal parts** with a damp rag.
3. **Dry thoroughly** to prevent rust.

### Deep Cleaning (Monthly or End of Season)
1. **Scrub** with a stiff wire brush to remove caked-on soil and sap.
2. **Soak stubborn residue** in warm, soapy water for 15 minutes, then scrub.
3. **Disinfect pruners and shears** with a solution of 1 part bleach to 9 parts water, or rubbing alcohol. This prevents disease transmission between plants.
4. **Dry completely** before storing.

## Sharpening

Sharp tools cut cleaner and require far less effort. Sharpen at least **twice per season** — more often with heavy use.

### Tools to Sharpen
- Pruners, loppers, and hedge shears
- Hoes and shovels (yes, shovels cut better when sharp!)
- Lawn mower blades

### How to Sharpen
1. **Secure the tool** in a vise or clamp.
2. Use a **flat mill file** for larger tools (shovels, hoes) and a **diamond file or whetstone** for pruners.
3. **Follow the original bevel angle** — typically 20–25 degrees.
4. **File in one direction only** — push strokes, not back-and-forth.
5. **Remove the burr** on the back side with a few light strokes.

## Rust Prevention

1. After cleaning and drying, apply a **light coat of linseed oil or WD-40** to all metal surfaces.
2. For long-term storage, coat metal parts with a thin layer of paste wax.
3. Store a **bucket of sand mixed with mineral oil** — plunge shovel and hoe blades into it to clean and oil simultaneously.

## Handle Care

- **Sand rough spots** with medium-grit sandpaper to prevent splinters.
- **Apply linseed oil** to wooden handles annually to prevent drying and cracking.
- **Replace cracked handles** immediately — a broken handle mid-use is a safety hazard.

## Storage

- **Hang tools** on wall-mounted racks or pegboards — don't pile them on the floor.
- **Store in a dry location** — a shed, garage, or covered area.
- **Keep blades off concrete floors** — moisture causes rust.

> **Tip:** Create a tool maintenance station with a wire brush, files, oil, and rags. Having supplies readily accessible makes routine care much more likely to happen.
