# How to Test and Adjust Soil pH

## Introduction

Soil pH is one of the most critical — yet frequently overlooked — factors in plant health. It controls nutrient availability: even if your soil is rich in nutrients, plants can't access them if the pH is wrong. A simple test and targeted amendments can transform your garden's performance.

## What is Soil pH?

- The pH scale ranges from **0 (extremely acidic) to 14 (extremely alkaline),** with 7 being neutral.
- Most garden plants thrive in **slightly acidic to neutral soil (pH 6.0–7.0).**
- **Acid-loving plants** (blueberries, azaleas, rhododendrons): Prefer pH 4.5–5.5.
- **Alkaline-tolerant plants** (lavender, clematis, lilac): Tolerate pH 7.0–8.0.

## Why pH Matters

At incorrect pH levels, essential nutrients become chemically "locked" in the soil:
- **Low pH (acidic):** Aluminum and manganese can reach toxic levels; phosphorus becomes unavailable.
- **High pH (alkaline):** Iron, manganese, and zinc become unavailable, causing chlorosis (yellowing leaves).

## How to Test

### DIY Home Test Kits
- Available at most garden centers. Affordable and easy to use.
- Mix soil with the provided solution and compare the color to the chart.
- Accuracy: ±0.5 pH units (good for general guidance).

### Digital pH Meters
- More precise and reusable. Insert the probe directly into moist soil.
- Calibrate regularly for accurate readings.

### Professional Lab Testing
- Most accurate option. Send a soil sample to your local cooperative extension office.
- Results include pH, nutrient levels, and specific amendment recommendations.
- Cost: Typically $15–$30.

## How to Adjust pH

### To Lower pH (Make More Acidic)
- **Eleite sulfur:** Slow-acting but long-lasting. Apply 1–2 lbs per 100 sq ft.
- **Iron sulfate:** Faster-acting but shorter duration.
- **Peat moss:** Mildly acidifying, also improves soil structure.
- **Pine needle mulch:** Very gradual acidifying effect.

### To Raise pH (Make More Alkaline)
- **Garden lime (calcium carbonate):** Most common method. Apply 5–10 lbs per 100 sq ft.
- **Dolomitic lime:** Adds both calcium and magnesium.
- **Wood ash:** Mild, fast-acting. Use sparingly — it can raise pH quickly.

## Important Notes

- pH changes take **several weeks to months** — don't expect instant results.
- **Test before amending** — never guess.
- **Retest annually** to monitor changes and maintain optimal levels.

> **Tip:** Test soil in multiple garden areas separately — pH can vary significantly even within a small yard.
