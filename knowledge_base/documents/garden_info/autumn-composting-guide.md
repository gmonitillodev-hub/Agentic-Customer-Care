# How to Start Composting in Fall

## Introduction

Autumn is the perfect time to start composting. The season provides an abundance of "brown" materials — fallen leaves, dead plants, and garden debris — that form the carbon-rich backbone of great compost. By spring, you'll have rich, dark compost ready to feed your garden.

## Why Fall is Ideal

- **Abundant materials:** Fallen leaves, spent garden plants, and grass clippings are plentiful.
- **Moderate temperatures:** Microbial activity is still strong in early fall.
- **Spring-ready compost:** A pile started in October can produce finished compost by April or May.

## Composting Basics: The Recipe

Good compost needs a balance of:

- **Browns (carbon):** Dried leaves, straw, cardboard, newspaper, wood chips. Provide energy for microbes.
- **Greens (nitrogen):** Kitchen scraps (fruit/vegetable peels, coffee grounds), fresh grass clippings, garden trimmings. Provide protein for microbes.
- **Ideal ratio:** Roughly **3 parts brown to 1 part green** by volume.
- **Water:** Compost should be as moist as a wrung-out sponge.
- **Air:** Turn or aerate regularly to prevent anaerobic (smelly) conditions.

## Setting Up Your Compost Bin

1. **Choose a location:** Level, well-drained spot with partial shade. Easy access year-round.
2. **Select a bin:** Tumbler, enclosed bin, wire mesh, or simple open pile. Enclosed bins retain heat better in cold weather.
3. **Start with a brown layer** (6 inches of leaves or straw) at the bottom for drainage.
4. **Alternate layers** of browns and greens.
5. **Moisten each layer** as you build.
6. **Cover the pile** to retain heat and moisture during fall rains.

## What NOT to Compost

- ❌ Meat, dairy, and oily foods (attract pests)
- ❌ Diseased plants (pathogens may survive)
- ❌ Pet waste (contains harmful bacteria)
- ❌ Treated or painted wood
- ❌ Weeds that have gone to seed

## Fall Composting Tips

- **Shred leaves** before adding — whole leaves mat together and decompose slowly. Run a mower over them.
- **Insulate the pile** with straw bales or extra leaves around the sides to maintain microbial heat through winter.
- **Continue adding kitchen scraps** throughout winter by burying them in the center of the pile.
- **Turn less frequently** in cold weather — once a month is sufficient.

> **Tip:** Keep a bag of shredded leaves near your compost bin. Every time you add kitchen scraps, toss in a handful of leaves to maintain the carbon-nitrogen balance.
