# When and How to Plant Garlic

## Introduction

Garlic is one of the easiest and most rewarding crops to grow at home. Planted in fall and harvested the following summer, it requires minimal attention during the growing season and rewards you with months of flavorful bulbs for the kitchen.

## Types of Garlic

- **Hardneck garlic:** Produces scapes (flower stalks), stronger flavor, fewer but larger cloves. Better for cold climates.
- **Softneck garlic:** No scapes, milder flavor, more cloves per bulb, stores longer. Ideal for braiding. Better for mild climates.
- **Elephant garlic:** Not true garlic (a type of leek), very mild flavor, massive cloves. Easy to grow.

## When to Plant

- **Fall planting (recommended):** 4–6 weeks before the ground freezes. This allows roots to establish before winter dormancy.
- **Typical timing:** October to November in most regions.
- **Spring planting:** Possible but produces smaller bulbs. Plant as soon as soil can be worked.

## How to Plant

1. **Select seed garlic** from a reputable source — not grocery store garlic, which may be treated to prevent sprouting.
2. **Break bulbs** into individual cloves just before planting. Keep the papery skin intact.
3. **Plant cloves** pointed end up, 2 inches deep and 6 inches apart.
4. **Rows** should be spaced 12 inches apart.
5. **Cover with soil** and water gently.
6. **Apply 4–6 inches of mulch** (straw or shredded leaves) to insulate over winter.

## Growing Season Care

- **Spring:** Remove excess mulch as shoots emerge. Begin watering regularly.
- **Scapes:** Snap off hardneck scapes when they curl — this directs energy to the bulb. (Bonus: scapes are delicious in stir-fries and pesto!)
- **Stop watering** 2–3 weeks before harvest to allow bulbs to cure.

## Harvesting & Curing

- Harvest when the **lower third of leaves** have turned brown (typically June–July).
- Cure in a warm, dry, well-ventilated area for 2–3 weeks.
- Trim roots and stems, then store in a cool, dark place.

> **Tip:** Save your largest, best bulbs as seed garlic for next year's planting.
